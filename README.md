## Overview

Hantu Jerangan is a Malay folkloric spirit reported in fishing communities along Malaysia's coasts. As with other hantu laut (sea spirits) and hantu-air-related entities, it functions as a supernatural explanation for unexplained drownings, lost fishermen, and dangerous conditions at sea, and reinforces traditional taboos and protective practices around fishing. The figure is distinct from better-attested Malay maritime spirits such as Hantu Laut, Hantu Jerambang, and Hantu Balung Bidai, but is commonly grouped with them as part of the broader Malay water-spirit complex. Available documentation in mainstream English-language sources is thin; most references are confined to community-level oral tradition and Malaysian-language folklore reportage. Where it is described, the entity is associated specifically with the dangers of fishing-net work and is invoked to caution fishermen against tangling, working alone, or transgressing customary maritime taboos.
